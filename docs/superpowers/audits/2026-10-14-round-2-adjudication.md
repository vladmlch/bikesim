Date: 2026-10-14. Wave-0 verification step of
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-14-round-2-integration-master.md` §3 WP-V4. Each of the
five assigned checks gets a verdict — `confirmed` / `refuted` / `partial` / `unresolved` —

## Check 1 — D4: what does the "~10 cm" in the WTA handbook actually mean?

**What was checked.** Gemini's track report cites "WTA (H_s ≈ 10 см)" as the basis for
the mobile scree-layer thickness on trafficked treads (Gemini track report, parameter
table "Сыпуха (`n_scree` / слой H_s)"), and the same source ("WTA *Terrain Dynamics*")
for repose percentages (talus 110 %, scree 80 %). The internal audit argued the 3–10 cm
veneer was never directly measured and that 4.1 cm mean / 12.9 cm max trail-incision
figures (Svajda et al.; Deogyusan 5.66 cm) are only proxies. The document behind the
"WTA" citation is Mike Shields, *An Introduction to Terrain for Trail Folks* (the
terrain-dynamics course handout distributed via Washington Trails Association). The full
text was rendered and OCR'd locally (`/tmp/wta-check/terrain_dynamics.pdf`, 35 pp).

**Verdict: partial** — the ~10 cm figure really exists in the cited source, but it is a
*construction* dimension, not a measured loose-layer thickness.

**Evidence.** Shields, p. 3 ("The Trail as a Structure"): *"The Structural Section is
the soil layer immediately beneath the Treadway, and it is given a section thickness
based on its ability to absorb and disperse the bearing and shear loads generated by the
trail traffic; this thickness is nominally 4 inches for pedestrian, stock and
mountain-bike traffic and 6 inches for all motorized traffic … On poorer soils or where
subsurface moisture is a problem, the Structural Section may be deepened to include an
aggregate Base Section."* 4 in = 10.16 cm — the source of Gemini's "≈10 см". It is the
nominal thickness of the engineered layer under a built treadway (a design rule for
load-spreading), not a measurement of mobile scree veneer thickness on natural slopes,
and not a drainage-layer spec. The scree 80 % repose figure (also from this document) is
a long-term repose table entry, unrelated to layer thickness.

**Consequence for the specification.** The audit position stands: the `H_s = 3–10 cm`
trafficked-tread veneer band keeps provenance `authored` — no measurement of mobile
veneer thickness on trafficked treads exists in any of the checked sources. The WTA
figure may be quoted only as "nominal structural-section thickness for built MTB treads
(4 in ≈ 10 cm, Shields/WTA terrain-dynamics handout, `standard`)" — a construction
analogy, acceptable as weak corroboration that ~10 cm is a plausible *order* for a
worked-on tread layer, never as a measured scree quantity.

## Check 2 — D6: "Salman 2021" vs "Alzubaidi et al. 2021", and the hard-bottom onset

**What was checked.** Whether the hard-layer pressure–sinkage model cited by the audit
("Salman 2021") and by Gemini ("Alzubaidi et al. 2021, Appl. Sci. 11:5499") is one work;
what the hardening formula is; and which of the three claimed onset depths is right:
audit ~1–1.5 stamp widths above the bottom, ChatGPT ~3 widths, Gemini `z_o = ζ_o·H_s`,
`ζ_o ≈ 0.35–0.55`.

**Verdict: partial** — the paper is uniquely identified and the formula confirmed;
"Alzubaidi" is a wrong author name for the same paper; none of the three onset
parametrisations is "the" measured law, but Salman's actual result supports the audit's
magnitude, not the other two.

**Evidence.** Appl. Sci. 11(12):5499 (DOI 10.3390/app11125499) is **Nihal D. Salman,
György Pillinger, Muammel M. Hanon, Péter Kiss, "A Modified Pressure–Sinkage Model for
Studying the Effect of a Hard Layer in Sandy Loam Soil"** (2021). No Alzubaidi-authored
2021 paper with this model exists; "Alzubaidi et al. 2021" in the Gemini report is a
misattribution of the same paper. Content (audit angle-A findings lines 18–52, 386–387;
paper Tables 2–4, §4): bevameter circular plate D = 0.20 m on sandy loam over a hard
layer at H = 0.30 and 0.40 m. Measured breaking point: **z_o/D = H/D − 1.05…1.07** —
the bottom is felt while the stamp is still ~1 stamp-diameter above it (compacted cone
reaches the bottom first). Below z_o ordinary power law; above it
`k_app = k + B·(e^{c·(z/D − z_o/D)} − 1)` with measured sandy-loam bands B = 10–20 N/cm²,
c = 1–3. The three disputed onsets resolve as follows:
- Audit `z_o ≈ H_s − (1.0–1.5)·b`: consistent with Salman's 1.06·D plus the Boussinesq
  influence-depth argument (line 386); at our b ≈ 55 mm, H_s = 3–10 cm it places the
  break at ≤ 0…~4 cm — i.e. the bottom matters almost immediately on thin layers.
- ChatGPT "~3 widths": confuses a different quantity — the Cerato & Lutenegger H/B ≤ 3
  threshold for when a rigid layer matters *at all* for bearing capacity (A findings
  line 230). Real, but answers "when does the bottom matter", not "where does p(z)
  break".
- Gemini `ζ_o·H_s = 0.35–0.55`: inside Salman's tested range it approximates the same
  point (H/D = 2 → z_o/H ≈ 0.47; H/D = 1.5 → ≈ 0.30), but it makes the *gap to the
  bottom* proportional to layer thickness, whereas Salman measured a constant ~1D gap.
  At our scale the two diverge hard: Gemini's TC-M2 uses z_o = 0.4·H_s = 14 mm, while
  Salman's form gives z_o = 35 − 1.06·58 < 0 mm. ζ_o is `authored` — not a measured
  constant.
Also `measured` only for sandy loam: B, c and the 1.06 coefficient are `extrapolated`
when applied to coarse scree (A findings line 405 flags exactly this).

**Consequence for the specification.** Cite **Salman et al. 2021** (correct the
"Alzubaidi" name wherever it propagates, incl. Gemini TC-M2's "Eq. (4)–(7)"). Adopt the
two-regime form with **`z_o = max(0, H_s − (1.0–1.5)·b)`** as default per the audit;
record ζ_o and the ~3B threshold as rejected alternatives with reasons. Mark
`B_hard`, `c_hard` `extrapolated` for gravel. The default implies: for H_s ≲ b·1.5 the
homogeneous-Bekker branch barely exists — the spec must not claim a bottom-free regime
for thin layers.

## Check 3 — golden-case arithmetic (Gemini TC-M1…M3 and audit-M G1–G12)

stated in the source itself*. Scripts:
`tools/research/adjudication/gemini_golden_cases.py` (TC-M1…M3) and
`tools/research/adjudication/audit_golden_cases.py` (G1–G9, G11, G12; G10 is a digitised
curve set without a single claimed value). Both ran under `uv run`; full outputs below.

reproduces; **TC-M1's work/coefficient numbers are wrong** (the audit's suspicion was

  with Prandtl closed form 30.14".

## Check 4 — Bulsink Table 5 values

**What was checked.** Which Table 5 numbers are correct — the old extraction
(216.07 / 1.011 / 21.444 / 20.510 per rad) or audit C's corrected
(−16.07 / 1.01 / −1.444 / −0.510 per rad). Verified twice, firsthand, against
`.scratch/tire-research/C/srcs/bulsink2015.pdf` (the `…_epub.pdf` is an HTML file, not a
usable table source).

**Verdict: confirmed** — the audit's corrected values are what the printed table says;
the old numbers are a pdftotext glyph-encoding artifact.

**Evidence.** (i) `pdftotext -f 19 -l 19` on the source PDF emits `216.07/rad`,
`1.011/rad`, `21.444/rad`, `20.510/rad` — the font's minus glyph maps to "2" in the text
layer. (ii) 400-dpi render + OCR of the same page recovers the semantic text:
`PKY1 Maximum value of cornering stiffness … −16.07/rad`; `PKY3 Peak cornering stiffness
factor 1.01 1/rad`; `PKY6 Camber stiffness factor −1.444/rad`; `PKY7 Vertical load
dependency of camber stiffness −0.510/rad` (C findings lines 159, 181 document the same
independently; provenance already marked `OCR-verified`, `cross_check_disputed` at
line 127). The signs are also physically consistent with the Pacejka conventions the
table defines (negative stiffness factors, positive peak factor) and with the Padova
data the fit is built on.

**Consequence for the specification.** Use `K_amax = −16.07 /rad`,
`K_apeak = 1.01`, `K_g1 = −1.444 /rad`, `K_g2 = −0.510 /rad` (Bulsink et al. 2015,
Table 5; provenance `OCR-verified`/`digitised`). Retire the corrupted values everywhere
they appear; note the lesson in the data-provenance convention — PDF text layers can lie
about signs, golden-table extraction needs rendered-page OCR or manual check. The
downstream use (C_γ/F_z ≈ 1.44 /rad upper edge) is unaffected since it already used the
corrected magnitude.

## Check 5 — patent US12565285B2

**What was checked.** Does the patent exist, what does it describe, and does it support
"production e-MTB traction control has no front-wheel speed sensor".

**Verdict: confirmed** (existence + content); the derived claim needs narrowing
(see consequence).

**Evidence.** US12565285B2, "Electrically propelled two-wheeled vehicle and method for
adjusting a drive torque of an electrically propelled two-wheeled vehicle", assignee
Robert Bosch GmbH; priority DE 10 2021 208 154.1 (2021-07-28); granted/published
2026-03-03; application US17/854,446; inventors Moia, Baumgaertner, Dauer, Reck, Klug
(Silas Klug = co-author of the 2019 Bosch wheelie paper stored in
`.scratch/track-research/g-emtb-reality/sources/klug2019_ebike_wheelie.pdf`).
Directly verified at patents.google.com. What it describes (abstract + J-2 summary):
anti-wheelie by pitch-rate — an inertial sensor yields the vehicle's pitch rate relative
to the surroundings; if the pitch rate produced by front-wheel lift exceeds a static
threshold, drive torque is reduced by a predefined amplification factor; J-2 adds the
Kalman fusion of 3D gyro + 3D accelerometer + rear odometry, gradient-compensation
threshold THR3, and "only reduce" filtering. Crucially, the patent's own background
states: on e-bikes a wheel-speed sensor "generates only one single measured value
(pulse) per wheel revolution" and is "generally provided exclusively at the rear wheel"
— contrasting motorcycles, where ABS puts sensors on both wheels.

**Consequence for the specification.** Cite US12565285B2 as primary evidence that (a) a
production-intent e-bike anti-wheelie controller runs on IMU pitch rate + rear-wheel
odometry only, and (b) the "1 pulse/rev, rear only" wheel-speed pickup is the typical
e-bike hardware configuration. Two scope limits must be written with it: the patent is
**anti-wheelie, not traction control** — slip-based TC claims rely on the separate
product documentation in J-1 (Bosch Dynamic Control/Traktionskontrolle, Specialized
Super TC, MAHLE M40); and the patent states what is *typical*, it does not prove no
e-MTB anywhere has a front sensor. So the spec may say "contemporary production e-MTB
TC/anti-wheelie architectures documented so far (Bosch patent + three OEM feature sets)
sense rider torque/cadence, rear speed and IMU — no front-wheel speed channel", not the
universal claim. For the testbench this is enough: the sensor set available to a
simulated controller should be {crank torque, cadence, rear-wheel speed, IMU
attitude/rates, gradient estimate}.

## Open questions / unresolved residue

1. **ζ_o band and TC-M2 parametrisation.** If the spec adopts `z_o = max(0, H_s − k·b)`,
  Gemini's golden TC-M2 (built on `z_o = ζ_o·H_s` with ζ_o = 0.40) needs its inputs
  re-derived under the adopted parametrisation before pinning; the *arithmetic* is fine,
  the *inputs* express a different model choice. Resolved-in-principle, but the golden
  must be regenerated after WP-S1 fixes the form.
2. **Slab-cap formula.** TC-M3 shows the two candidate slab formulas diverge by ≥ 40 %
  on moderate slopes and by ∞ on a repose slope (flat formula gives finite traction
  where resolved gives zero). Which form the spec pins is WP-S1/D15's decision; this
  audit only establishes that the printed TC-M3 value is the flat-ground form.
  A field spin-out-slope test remains the deciding experiment.
3. **"Alzubaidi" chain of custody.** No separate Alzubaidi et al. 2021 hard-layer paper
  was found; if a source other than Appl. Sci. 11:5499 was meant, it has not been
  located. Treat as closed (misattribution of Salman et al.) unless someone produces
  the alternate paper.
4. **G10** (Ding 2011 digitised curves) was not re-digitised — it is a multi-point
  `digitised` reference, not a golden number; re-check belongs with the lateral/
  digitisation wave (WP-V3-style pipeline), not here.
5. **WTA attribution chain.** The Shields handout circulates under WTA's training
  materials but carries no in-document WTA imprint in the copy examined; the citation
  "WTA Terrain Dynamics" is Gemini's shorthand. Attribution detail only — does not
  change the Check-1 verdict.
