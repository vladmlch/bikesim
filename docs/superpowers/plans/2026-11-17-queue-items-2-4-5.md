- Produces: Exhaustive research report justifying the 1750 N ceiling as the upper physical limit of seated riding.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write `docs/research/2026-11-17-saddle-impact-tolerance.md`**

Structure:
1. Executive Summary & Conclusion: Why 1750 N (~2.23g for 80 kg rider) is the biologically sound validity boundary for a seated riding model.
2. Static vs Dynamic Seated Saddle Loads:
   - Baseline seated weight distribution: 40–50% of body weight (~315–400 N for 80 kg).
   - Dynamic power unweighting: ~5.2% reduction in saddle load per 1 W/kg increase in crank power (Kram et al., Carahalios 2015).
3. Shock & Vibration Transmissibility on Rough Terrain:
   - Measured vertical accelerations ($g$-forces) at the saddle in off-road cycling: peak values typically 2.0g–3.5g (Chiumente et al., Bovenzi / ISO 2631).
   - Dynamic normal force calculation on bump impact: $F_{\text{peak}} = m_{\text{eff}} \cdot (g + a_z)$. For 80 kg rider with effective seated mass $m_{\text{eff}} \approx 56\text{ kg}$, a 2.2g shock produces $\approx 1750\text{ N}$.
4. Human Tolerance and the Sit-to-Stand Transition:
   - Impact testing standards for saddles (ISO 4210-9, *BIKE* magazine rig tests): 1500 N peak impact loads.
   - Pain, soft-tissue injury, and reflex unloading: impacts exceeding 1200–1500 N trigger immediate transition out of the saddle (Costes et al. 2015). Riders refuse to stay seated when shock exceeds ~1.5–1.8 kN.
5. Application to Project Decisions:
   - Justifies `weld_saddle_max_normal_n = 1750.0 N` (decision S.1).
   - Explains why `rough_uphill_extreme` (peak 1378 N) is valid seated riding, while `alpine_rocky` (peak 1842 N) exceeds human seated tolerance and requires standing.
