# FP32 physics experiment — drift comparison & verdict memo

Date: 2026-10-15 · Worktree: `.worktrees/fp32-physics` (branch `research/fp32-physics`)
Inputs: `output/fp32-probe/probe-f64-8x4000.json` (FP64 oracle lane),
`output/fp32-probe/probe-f32-8x4000.json` (FP32 lane) — 8 episodes × 4000 steps
(0.5 ms) each, same ingested state + input trace, perturbation schedule
`qpos += i·1e-4` on `fork_travel`/`shock_stroke`, `qvel *= 1 + i·1e-5`.
Tooling: `tools/fp32_probe/compare.py` → `output/fp32-probe/compare.json` /
`compare.md` / `compare.png` (all under gitignored `output/`).

**Comparison contract** (verbatim from the probes' provenance): *"timing and
statistical drift only; no bitwise claim is made across precisions (the
bitwise FP64 contract is untouched)."* Nothing in this memo asserts or
implies cross-precision bitwise equivalence — the FP64 oracle and its
bitwise contract are unchanged.

**Scope.** This measures the *native C++ runtime* (`bike_native_exp`
first-party writers + MuJoCo 3.12.0) compiled under `-DmjUSESINGLE`
(`mjtNum=float`, `BIKE_FP32`) on Apple silicon, single-threaded, sequential
episodes. It does **not** measure MJWarp, GPU throughput, or Python-side
costs.

## 3. Component-level divergence (actionable detail)

First-diverging writers (onset = first step where `max|Δ|` exceeds
max(1e-4·FP64 scale, 1e-5)): **`closure` and `ideal_transmission` — both at
k=0 in every episode.** The FP32 lane's first-step outputs already differ:

- `ideal_transmission` at k=0: FP64 emits exact zeros while FP32 emits

  2.2e-3–4.9e-3 rel across episodes (worst ep6, 4.88e-3).
- Rear tire (ep0): `slip_mps` peak rel 3.4e-2 / terminal 8.6e-4 (worst
  terminal rel across episodes 3.5e-3, ep6); `normal_load_n` peak rel
  3.7e-3. `slip_ratio`/`support_n` are emitted as
  0 on every row in both lanes — **unexercised channels** in this workload;
  the slip verdict rests on `slip_mps` + `normal_load_n`.
- No crashes, no balance loss, no flag mismatches anywhere.

Additional input-level asymmetry worth stating: the FP32 lane integrates
with dt = float32(0.0005) = 0.0005000000237 (+2.4e-11 absolute, +4.7e-8
relative). dt quantization alone explains only 4000×2.4e-11 ≈ 9.5e-8 s of
`time_s` drift — the observed uniform 6.74e-5 s is dominated by per-step
float32 **accumulation** rounding in `d->time += h` (ulp(t) ≈ 2.4e-7 at
t ≈ 2.2 s, a sub-ulp rounding at each of 4000 adds; the FP32 clock ends
~6.7e-5 s behind). Verified against the traces: FP64 `time_s` is exactly
t0 + k·0.0005, while FP32 `time_s` reproduces a simulated float32 running
sum — the identical |Δ| in all 8 episodes is deterministic accumulation,
not noise.

## 8. The call

**Verdict: ACCEPTABLE IN RANGE → proceed to the Colab/GPU throughput gate;
the native-CPU-precision question itself is closed with "no benefit".**

Range X (2 s of violent rough-terrain riding, 8 perturbation episodes):

- `max|Δqpos|` terminal: median 8e-4, worst 9.2e-3 (still growing at cutoff);
  peak over the run 1.02e-2 (ep0 @ k=3203).
- `max|Δqvel|` terminal: median 5.7e-3, worst 4.4e-2.
- Force/energy channels: sustained ~1e-4–1e-3 relative; transient peaks up
  to ~1.4% (closure), ~3% (slip_mps), ~33% (mid_drive electrical transient).
- Zero flag/crash/balance/attachment mismatches; no solver-iteration
  inflation.

Justification:

1. **Noise ≪ designed signal.** Cross-lane drift is 0.8–24% of the
   within-lane spread produced by the smallest IC perturbation (1e-4 m) and
   orders below the larger ones — the system's response to FP32 is smaller
   than its response to a sub-millimetre initial-condition nudge. For
   statistical workloads (the MJWarp use case) that is the "acceptable" bar.
2. **Bounded, not runaway.** Growth is sub-exponential everywhere, peaks at
   the rough section, partially recovers — no chaotic blow-up inside 2 s.
   ep6's still-growing 9.2e-3 terminal is the worst bound, not a divergence.
3. **One systematic caveat, honestly stated:** constraint-boundary
   bookkeeping diverges — the freehub-limit tendon engages ~47% more often
   under FP32 in every episode, and force-path components differ ~1% from
   step 0. Trajectory distributions stay matched; event-level constraint
   classification does not. If a downstream use needs constraint-event
   reproducibility, this is the number to tighten first.
4. **No native speedup.** Median µs/step is 1.4% *slower* under FP32
   (mean parity once the FP64 outlier is excluded). Whatever FP32 buys, it
   is not CPU wall-clock on this workload — the throughput case lives
   entirely on the GPU/Colab side, which this experiment cannot measure.

Caveats on interpretation: measured drift bundles the declared
`opt.tolerance` asymmetry (1e-6 vs 1e-10) with pure rounding — an
FP64@1e-6-tolerance control lane would separate the two contributions.
`slip_ratio`/`support_n` are unexercised channels in this trace. The 2 s
horizon leaves ep6's tail unbounded — a longer run would firm up the
worst-case number.

If the gate needs tightening before Colab: (i) FP64@`opt.tolerance`=1e-6
control run; (ii) longer episode (≥10 s) to bound the ep6 tail; (iii) a
workload that exercises `slip_ratio`/`support_n`; (iv) bootstrap-restored
writer state to remove the shared settle transient.
